# Advanced Topics in Classical Chinese

This advanced curriculum is designed for learners who have completed foundational training in Classical Chinese grammar, syntax, and canon, and are ready to specialize in advanced Sinological subfields. Rather than attempting to complete every track, you should select one or two tracks aligned with your specific research interests, whether in primary manuscript analysis, historical phonology, traditional philology, regional Sinitic registers, or computational methods.

- [Paleography, Epigraphy, and Early Excavated Texts](#paleography-epigraphy-and-early-excavated-texts) - Deciphers archaic script stages on oracle bones, ritual bronze vessels, and Warring States bamboo and silk manuscripts.
- [Historical Phonology and Sinological Linguistics](#historical-phonology-and-sinological-linguistics) - Reconstructs the phonetic systems, rhyming categories, and syntactic transformations from Old Chinese through Middle Chinese.
- [Textual Criticism, Philology, and Commentarial Exegesis](#textual-criticism-philology-and-commentarial-exegesis) - Teaches traditional and modern methods of textual collation (jiaokanxue), Qing evidential scholarship (kaozhengxue), and canonical commentarial layers.
- [Medieval Sinitic, Buddhist Translation, and the Sinosphere](#medieval-sinitic-buddhist-translation-and-the-sinosphere) - Explores non-standard registers, Buddhist translation syntax, Dunhuang vernacular manuscripts, and Literary Sinitic across East Asia.
- [Digital Sinology, Computational Philology, and Spatial Analysis](#digital-sinology-computational-philology-and-spatial-analysis) - Applies programmatic text mining, named-entity recognition, prosopographical database querying, and historical GIS to classical textual corpora.

## Paleography, Epigraphy, and Early Excavated Texts

This track trains researchers to read, transcribe, and analyze primary archaeological materials prior to script standardization into clerical and standard scripts.

Oracle Bone Inscriptions (Jiaguwen): [Sources of Shang History: The Oracle-Bone Inscriptions of Bronze Age China (David N. Keightley, University of California Press)](https://archive.org/details/sourcesofshanghi0000keig) - The canonical manual for deciphering divination cracks, reading Shang grammatical formulas, and interpreting the earliest extant Chinese script.

Bronze Epigraphy (Jinwen): [Sources of Western Zhou History: Inscribed Bronze Vessels (Edward L. Shaughnessy, University of California Press)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780520070288) - A foundational guide to reading Western Zhou bronze inscriptions, detailing vessel types, formulaic grammar, dating methods, and ritual rhetoric.

Warring States Bamboo and Silk Manuscripts: [Rewriting Early Chinese Texts (Edward L. Shaughnessy, State University of New York Press)](https://archive.org/details/rewritingearlych0000shau) - An essential study on how excavated bamboo strips alter our understanding of textual formation, scribal transmission, and early philosophical variation.

Paleographic Script Evolution: [Chinese Writing (Qiu Xigui, trans. Gilbert L. Mattos and Jerry Norman, Society for the Study of Early China)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9781557290717) - The definitive masterwork on Chinese paleography, structural analysis of character formation, graphic variation, and phonetic loan usage across script stages.

## Historical Phonology and Sinological Linguistics

This track investigates the sound systems, archaic rhyming categories, and syntactic evolution of the Chinese language from early antiquity through the medieval period.

Middle Chinese Phonology and Rhyme Tables: [Middle Chinese: A Study in Historical Phonology (Edwin G. Pulleyblank, UBC Press)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780774801928) - The foundational monograph analyzing the Qieyun tradition, fanqie spelling, rhyme tables, and reconstructed phonetic values of medieval Chinese.

Old Chinese Reconstruction and Archaic Rhymes: [Old Chinese: A New Reconstruction (William H. Baxter and Laurent Sagart, Oxford University Press)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780199945375) - The authoritative modern six-vowel reconstruction of early Chinese phonology, root morphology, and Shijing rhyme categories.

Old Chinese Reconstruction and Archaic Rhymes: [Chinese Historical Phonology (Guillaume Jacques and Nathan W. Hill, Leiden University / YouTube)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CBkjZAfvIBA) - An open recorded lecture series providing systematic university instruction on internal reconstruction, phonetic series (xiesheng), and Old Chinese onsets and codas.

Historical Syntax and Classical Semantics: [Language and Logic (Christoph Harbsmeier, Cambridge University Press)](https://archive.org/details/science-civilisation-china-vol-7-part-1) - Volume 7, Part 1 of Joseph Needham's Science and Civilisation in China, providing an exhaustive account of classical grammar, quantification, negation, and semantic logic.

## Textual Criticism, Philology, and Commentarial Exegesis

This track equips scholars with traditional and modern philological methodologies to evaluate woodblock editions, identify scribal corruption, and navigate classical commentarial layers.

Textual Collation and Edition Studies (Jiaokanxue): [Chinese History: A New Manual (Endymion Wilkinson, Harvard University Asia Center)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780674067158) - The indispensable Sinological manual whose chapters on traditional book culture, collation techniques, and error typology establish practical philological competence.

Qing Evidential Scholarship (Kaozhengxue): [From Philosophy to Philology: Intellectual and Social Aspects of Change in Late Imperial China (Benjamin A. Elman, Harvard University Asia Center)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780674005914) - The definitive intellectual history analyzing how high-Qing scholars developed rigorous phonological, epigraphic, and historical-critical methods to recover ancient meanings.

Commentarial Systems of the Thirteen Classics: [The Five "Confucian" Classics (Michael Nylan, Yale University Press)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780300081855) - An authoritative structural analysis of the core canon, tracing how Han-Tang subcommentaries (shu) and Song Neo-Confucian commentaries transformed textual interpretation.

## Medieval Sinitic, Buddhist Translation, and the Sinosphere

This track explores Classical Chinese as a dynamic transnational medium, focusing on Buddhist translation registers, Dunhuang vernacular manuscripts, and Literary Sinitic across East Asia.

Buddhist Translation Idioms: [A Guide to the Earliest Chinese Buddhist Translations (Jan Nattier, International Research Institute for Advanced Buddhology, Soka University)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9784904234006) - A masterly philological guide establishing precise lexical and syntactical criteria for analyzing early medieval translation idioms and dating texts.

Buddhist Translation Idioms: [The Buddhist Conquest of China: The Spread and Adaptation of Buddhism in Early Medieval China (Erik Zürcher, Brill)](https://archive.org/details/the-buddhist-conquest-of-china) - The landmark study examining the cultural and linguistic adaptation of Indian Buddhist concepts into early medieval Chinese prose.

Dunhuang Vernacular Manuscripts: [T'ang Transformation Texts (Victor H. Mair, Harvard University Asia Center)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780674868151) - A seminal monograph analyzing the rise of vernacularized narrative prose (bianwen) and colloquial registers preserved in the Dunhuang manuscript cache.

Literary Sinitic in the Sinosphere: [Languages, Scripts, and Chinese Texts in East Asia (Peter Kornicki, Oxford University Press)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780198797821) - An indispensable cross-regional study exploring how Classical Chinese operated as a shared literary medium in Japan, Korea, and Vietnam alongside vernacular reading practices.

Literary Sinitic in the Sinosphere: [An Introduction to Japanese Kanbun (Akira Komai and Thomas H. Rohlich, University of Nagoya Press)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9784930689849) - A practical, structured textbook teaching the syntactical reordering marks (kunten) and Japanese glossing techniques (kundoku) used to read Classical Chinese throughout East Asian history.

## Digital Sinology, Computational Philology, and Spatial Analysis

This track introduces computational methods for analyzing pre-modern Chinese texts, including programmatic text mining, named-entity extraction, prosopographical network analysis, and historical GIS.

Programmatic Text Mining and Corpus APIs: [Classical Chinese Digital Humanities Tutorials (Donald Sturgeon, Digital Sinology)](https://dsturgeon.net/tutorials/) - A free series of interactive tutorials and Python Jupyter notebooks designed specifically for humanities scholars to query classical corpora via the Chinese Text Project API.

Named Entity Recognition and Automated Tagging: [MARKUS: Text Analysis and Reading Platform (Hilde De Weerdt and Brent Ho, Leiden University)](https://dh.chinese-empires.eu/markus/) - A specialized digital platform with integrated video tutorials for automated extraction and markup of personal names, official titles, and place names in classical texts.

Prosopography and Historical Social Networks: [CBDB Computational Methods for Chinese History (China Biographical Database Project, Harvard University / YouTube)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XQGJKmljiik) - A recorded university workshop providing step-by-step instruction on querying the China Biographical Database, exporting relational datasets, and conducting social network analysis.

Historical GIS and Spatial Infrastructure: [China Historical GIS (Harvard University and Fudan University)](https://chgis.fas.harvard.edu/) - A free spatial data infrastructure and methodological clearinghouse for mapping historical administrative units and territorial changes across imperial dynasties.
