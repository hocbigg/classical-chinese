# Great Readings in Classical Chinese

This curated reading list gathers field-defining monographs, seminal theoretical essays, and landmark philological studies in Classical Chinese. Moving beyond introductory pedagogical grammars, these works illuminate how master scholars decipher ancient scripts, reconstruct archaic phonology and syntax, interpret classical aesthetics, and analyze the transnational spread of Literary Sinitic.

## Early Thought, Argumentation, and Philosophical Syntax

| Title | Author(s) | Type | Why It's Worth Reading |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| *[Disputers of the Tao: Philosophical Argument in Ancient China](https://archive.org/details/disputersoftaoph0000grah_z2m6)* | A. C. Graham | Monograph | The definitive intellectual history of Pre-Qin thought, integrating rigorous linguistic analysis with philosophical reconstruction of Warring States debates. |
| *[Language and Logic](https://archive.org/details/science-civilisation-china-vol-7-part-1)* (Science and Civilisation in China, Vol. 7, Part 1) | Christoph Harbsmeier | Monograph | A monumental comparative investigation of semantic categorization, explicit logic, rhetoric, and grammatical sentence structures in classical texts. |
| *[Language and Logic in Ancient China](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL4157833W)* | Chad Hansen | Monograph | Introduced the influential mass-noun hypothesis and a non-representational semantic theory of Classical Chinese that revolutionized comparative philosophy. |
| *[Confucius: The Secular as Sacred](https://archive.org/details/confuciusthesecul00fing)* | Herbert Fingarette | Monograph | A seminal philosophical essay analyzing the performative, non-psychologized nature of ritual (*li*) and language in the *Analects*. |
| *[Thinking Through Confucius](https://archive.org/details/thinkingthroughc0000hall)* | David L. Hall, Roger T. Ames | Monograph | Proposes an influential interpretive framework contrasting Western transcendental dualism with the contextual, immanent cosmology of Classical Chinese thinking. |

## Philology, Paleography, and Textual Stratification

| Title | Author(s) | Type | Why It's Worth Reading |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| *[Chinese Writing](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL15187600W)* | Qiu Xigui (trans. Gilbert L. Mattos, Jerry Norman) | Monograph | The premier authority on the evolution of early Chinese scripts, covering oracle bones, bronze inscriptions, clerical transformation, and graphic variants. |
| *[Rewriting Early Chinese Texts](https://archive.org/details/rewritingearlych0000shau)* | Edward L. Shaughnessy | Monograph | Demonstrates how newly discovered bamboo and silk manuscripts challenge the transmission history, composition dates, and editorial integrity of received classics. |
| *[From Philosophy to Philology: Intellectual and Social Aspects of Change in Late Imperial China](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL2965476W)* | Benjamin A. Elman | Monograph | A landmark study detailing the rise of the High-Qing evidential research (*kaozheng*) movement and its revolution in rigorous historical linguistics and epigraphy. |
| *[The Origin and Early Development of the Chinese Writing System](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL3193077W)* | William G. Boltz | Monograph | Proposes a comprehensive structural and phonophoric theory of how logographic writing emerged and stabilized in Archaic China. |
| *[Sources of Western Zhou History: Inscribed Bronze Vessels](https://books.google.com/books?id=BH1ZrCsslosC)* | Edward L. Shaughnessy | Monograph | An indispensable guide to dating, transcribing, and interpreting the complex legal, historical, and ritual syntax of Western Zhou bronze epigraphy (*jinwen*). |

## Poetics, Hermeneutics, and Literary Form

| Title | Author(s) | Type | Why It's Worth Reading |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| *[Traditional Chinese Poetry and Poetics: Omen of the World](https://archive.org/details/traditionalchine0000owen)* | Stephen Owen | Monograph | A foundational theoretical work arguing that Classical Chinese poetry operates as an unmediated, non-fictional response to the world rather than Western mimesis or allegory. |
| *[The Propensity of Things: Toward a History of Efficacy in China](https://archive.org/details/propensityofthin0000jull)* | François Jullien (trans. Janet Lloyd) | Monograph | An incisive exploration of *shi* (situational potential and structural momentum) as an organizing aesthetic and strategic concept across classical literature, art, and statecraft. |
| *[The Art of Chinese Poetry](https://archive.org/details/artofchinesepoet0000liuj)* | James J. Y. Liu | Monograph | A classic structural and stylistic study examining how the monosyllabic nature of characters, tonal meter, and syntactic omission generate layered poetic resonance. |
| *[The End of the Chinese 'Middle Ages': Essays on Mid-Tang Literary Culture](https://books.google.com/books?id=CcaKtAEACAAJ)* | Stephen Owen | Monograph | Explores the intellectual and psychological shifts that sparked the Ancient-Style Prose (*Guwen*) movement and redefined literary individuality during the Tang-Song transition. |
| *[The Problem of a Chinese Aesthetic](https://www.sup.org/books/title/?id=1409)* | Haun Saussy | Monograph | A sophisticated comparative inquiry into early commentary traditions, exploring whether the *Shijing* (Classic of Poetry) can be understood through Western concepts of allegory. |

## Sinitic Transnationalism, Translation, and Comparative Philology

| Title | Author(s) | Type | Why It's Worth Reading |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| *[Languages, Scripts, and Chinese Texts in East Asia](https://academic.oup.com/book/7521)* | Peter Kornicki | Monograph | The benchmark study on Literary Sinitic as the common written language of pre-modern East Asia, detailing vernacular glossing and reading systems in Japan, Korea, and Vietnam. |
| *[Late Han Vernacular Elements in the Earliest Buddhist Translations](https://brill.com/display/book/edcoll/9789004263291/B9789004263291_003.xml)* | Erik Zürcher | Landmark Paper | A groundbreaking philological paper that isolated hybrid vernacular syntax and colloquial loan expressions in early Han-period Buddhist translations. |
| *[19 Ways of Looking at Wang Wei (with More Ways)](https://www.ndbooks.com/book/19-ways-of-looking-at-wang-wei/)* | Eliot Weinberger, Octavio Paz | Book | A concise, brilliant comparative demonstration of how the absence of tense, number, and personal pronouns in Classical Chinese verse challenges Western translation. |
| *[The Buddhist Conquest of China: The Spread and Adaptation of Buddhism in Early Medieval China](https://archive.org/details/the-buddhist-conquest-of-china)* | Erik Zürcher | Monograph | The classic account of how Indian Buddhist concepts and foreign Sanskrit terminology were synthetically translated and integrated into medieval Chinese gentry culture. |
