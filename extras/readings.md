# Great Readings in Classical Chinese

This curated reading list gathers field-defining monographs, seminal theoretical essays, and landmark philological studies in Classical Chinese. Moving beyond introductory pedagogical grammars, these works illuminate how master scholars decipher ancient scripts, reconstruct archaic phonology and syntax, interpret classical aesthetics, and analyze the transnational spread of Literary Sinitic.

## Early Thought, Argumentation, and Philosophical Syntax

These readings examine how early Chinese thinkers conceptualized truth, dialectic, and ritual action, paying close attention to the philosophical grammar, semantic categorization, and rhetorical strategies of Pre-Qin texts.

| Title | Author(s) |
| :--- | :--- |
| [*Disputers of the Tao: Philosophical Argument in Ancient China*](https://archive.org/details/disputersoftaoph0000grah_z2m6) | A. C. Graham |
| [*Language and Logic* (Science and Civilisation in China, Vol. 7, Part 1)](https://archive.org/details/science-civilisation-china-vol-7-part-1) | Christoph Harbsmeier |
| [*Language and Logic in Ancient China*](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9781938421549) | Chad Hansen |
| [*Confucius: The Secular as Sacred*](https://archive.org/details/confuciusthesecul00fing) | Herbert Fingarette |
| [*Thinking Through Confucius*](https://archive.org/details/thinkingthroughc0000hall) | David L. Hall, Roger T. Ames |
